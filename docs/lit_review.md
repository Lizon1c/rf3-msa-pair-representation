## 1. Why the MSA was introduced: from coevolution to the Evoformer

The MSA entered structure prediction as the carrier of **evolutionary covariation**. Residue pairs that co-vary across a protein family are likely to be in contact; methods based on direct coupling analysis (DCA / EVcouplings) could predict contacts and even fold proteins **without any supervised structural data**. As the OpenFold discussion puts it: "MSAs implicitly encode a substantial amount of structural knowledge, as evidenced by early coevolution-based structure prediction methods that were entirely unsupervised, making no use of experimental structural data" [24, Discussion]. AlphaFold2's own reference list anchors this lineage with Marks, Hopf & Sander, "Protein structure prediction from sequence variation" [9] (cited as ref. 41 in [1], p7).

**AlphaFold1** (Senior et al. 2020 [8]) converted coevolutionary statistics from an MSA into deep-learned distance potentials; **AlphaFold2** (Jumper et al. 2021 [1]) made the MSA a first-class, actively processed input. The core block, the Evoformer, operates on two coupled arrays: a "network block that we term Evoformer to produce an Nseq × Nres array … that represents a processed MSA and an Nres × Nres array that represents residue pairs. The MSA representation is initialized with the raw MSA" [1, p3]. "The columns of the MSA representation encode the individual residues of the input sequence while the rows represent the sequences in which those residues appear" [1, p3]. MSA and template information enter as **separate input tracks** — the ablation panel of Fig. 3 lists independent conditions "No templates", "No raw MSA (use MSA pairwise frequencies)", and "No auxiliary masked MSA head" [1, p5].

The MSA is also wired into **training**, not just inference: the architecture uses "masked MSA loss to jointly train with the structure" [1, p3], implemented as follows: "we use random masking on the input MSAs and require the network to reconstruct the masked regions from the output MSA representation using a BERT-like loss" [1, Methods, p8]. The rationale is to force the MSA representation to carry enough information to rebuild evolutionary statistics, tying the MSA channel to the structure objective.

Critically for everything that follows, the AF2 authors already documented the MSA's **dose–response** and its **role split**: "the accuracy decreases substantially when the median alignment depth is less than around 30 sequences … We observe a threshold effect where improvements in MSA depth over around 100 sequences lead to small gains. We hypothesize that the MSA information is needed to coarsely find the correct structure within the early stages of the network, but refinement of that prediction into a high-accuracy model does not depend crucially on the MSA information" [1, Discussion, p6]. The Methods repeat the threshold: "the depth of the MSA is relatively unimportant until it approaches a threshold value of around 30 sequences when the MSA size effects become quite large" [1, p8]. And the training target is explicitly crystallographic: "AlphaFold is trained to produce the protein structure most likely to appear as part of a PDB structure" [1, Discussion, p6]. These three sentences — early-stage MSA dependence, a ~30-sequence floor with ~100-sequence saturation, and a PDB-most-likely training objective — are the seeds of every bias result in §3.

## 2. The de-emphasis and removal arc: ESMFold → ESM3 → AF3 → RF3

**ESMFold (Lin et al. 2022 [3])** removed the MSA outright. The approach produces "atomic resolution structure prediction directly from sequence … eliminating the need for a multiple sequence alignment, while at the same time greatly simplifying the neural architecture used for inference. This results in an improvement in speed of up to 60x on the inference forward pass alone" [3, p2; abstract p1]. Architecturally, "Because we do not have an MSA, the modules responsible for processing the MSA are no longer necessary" [3, p21]. The key empirical claims: (i) "When evaluating AlphaFold and RosettaFold on single sequences by ablating the multiple sequence alignment, performance degrades substantially, and falls well below that of ESMFold" [3, p6] — i.e., the MSA-processing machinery is useless, even harmful, without the MSA it was designed for; (ii) accuracy parity on easy targets: ESMFold matches AlphaFold2 within 0.05 LDDT on more than half of proteins, and "parts of structure with low accuracy do not differ significantly between ESMFold and AlphaFold, suggesting that language models are learning information similar to the contents of MSAs" [3, p7]; (iii) the regime where the MSA still matters is the shallow-MSA/orphan regime, where ESMFold was benchmarked on proteins with "104 sequences with MSA depth ≤ 100, 70 sequences with MSA depth ≤ 10, and 22 sequences with MSA depth = 1" [3, Appendix, p22]. Speed: "ESMFold makes a prediction on a protein with 384 residues in 14.2 seconds, 6x faster than a single AlphaFold2 model. On shorter sequences the improvement increases up to ∼60x" [3, p6].

**ESM3 (Hayes et al. 2024 [4])** extended the single-sequence paradigm to a generative model over sequence, structure and function tokens — still with **no MSA input**: "in single sequence structure prediction ESM3 98B surpasses ESMFold (0.880 vs 0.861 mean local distance difference test, LDDT, CAMEO test set; Table S9)" [4, p4]. Its Table S9 is the cleanest available measurement of how much the MSA is still worth at the structure level: AlphaFold2 scores 0.903 / 0.857 / 0.833 LDDT on CAMEO / CASP14 / CASP15, versus ESM3-98B 0.880 / 0.724 / 0.787 and ESMFold 0.861 / 0.722 / 0.745 [4, Table S9, p44]. The MSA-backed model's advantage is small on the easy continuous-release set (CAMEO: +0.023 over ESM3-98B) and concentrated on the hard CASP targets (CASP14: +0.133; CASP15: +0.046) — the MSA is a hard-target resource, not a uniform one.

**AlphaFold3 (Abramson et al. 2024 [2])** kept the MSA but architecturally demoted it. "Within the trunk, MSA processing is substantially de-emphasized, with a much smaller and simpler MSA embedding block" [2, p2]. Concretely: "Compared with the original evoformer from AF2, the number of blocks is reduced to four, the processing of the MSA representation uses an inexpensive pair-weighted averaging and only the pair representation is used for later processing steps" [2, p2]. The MSA no longer survives as a representation at all: the Pairformer "operates only on the pair representation and the single representation; the MSA representation is not retained and all information passes through the pair representation" [2, p2]. In other words, in AF3 the MSA is a **one-shot write into the pair representation** — exactly the channel our project isolates in RF3 (§5). Yet the information still matters: "AF3 has a very similar dependence on MSA depth to AlphaFold-Multimer v.2.3; proteins with shallow MSAs are predicted with lower accuracy" [2, p5]. And for small-molecule interactions the authors claim the MSA is dispensable: the improvement in antibody and protein–ligand "results suggests AlphaFold-derived methods are able to model the chemistry and physics of classes of molecular interactions without dependence on MSAs" [2, Discussion, p8].

**RosettaFold-3 (Corley et al. 2025 [5])**, the open implementation our project uses, "began by implementing the algorithms described in the Supplemental Methods of the AlphaFold3 (AF3) manuscript" [5, Appendix A.3.2, p19] and "narrows the performance gap between closed-source AlphaFold3 (AF3) and existing open-source implementations" [5, Abstract, p1]. Its input list treats the MSA as one featurized track among several: "Inputs to RF3 include the primary polymer sequence, evolutionary information (both protein and RNA MSAs), atom-level ground-truth templates (a generalization of AF2-style protein templates), reference conformers generated with RDKit … and ML force field embeddings (optional)" [5, Appendix A.3.1, p18], with templates embedded separately "via the token-level template track, binning pairwise distances into 64 distance bins" [5, p18]. Notably, **the paper contains no ablation removing the MSA input**: the only ablation-related statement in the entire manuscript is "We did not directly ablate the impact on training of using MSAs from HHBlits vs. MMSeqs2-GPU" [5, p18] (a search-tool comparison, not an MSA-presence ablation). The functional contribution of the MSA to an AF3-style trunk is therefore unmeasured in the RF3 paper itself — one of the gaps this review documents (§5).

**RFdiffusion (Watson et al. 2023 [6])** closes the arc: a generative model in which the MSA is **never used** — the terms "MSA" and "multiple sequence alignment" occur zero times in the paper. The structural prior is carried entirely by pretrained weights: "Fine-tuning RFdiffusion from pretrained RF weights was far more successful than training for an equivalent length of time from untrained weights" [6, p2]; "RFdiffusion leverages the representations learned during RF pre-training. RFdiffusion fine-tuned from pre-trained RF … comprehensively outperforms a model trained for an equivalent amount of time, from untrained weights" [6, Extended Data Fig. 1 caption, p15]; and the pretraining ablation is decisive: "Ablating pretraining (by starting from untrained RF)…" yields zero in-silico success [6, Fig. 2e caption, p4].

**Summary of the arc.** In four years the field moved from "MSA as coevolutionary engine" (AF2 Evoformer) → "MSA eliminable by a language model" (ESMFold/ESM3) → "MSA collapsed into a 4-block, pair-weighted write into the pair representation" (AF3, and identically RF3) → "no MSA at all; the prior lives in pretrained weights" (RFdiffusion). The de-emphasis is architectural, not informational: both AF2 and AF3 retain a measurable MSA-depth dependence [1, p6; 2, p5], and the depth at which it saturates (~30–100 sequences [1, p6]) is strikingly shallow — consistent with our finding that 8–15 MSA rows already install the full effect in RF3 (§5).

## 3. Evidence that deep MSA pins predictors to single / crystallized conformations

### 3.1 The MSA-perturbation family

A consistent experimental result since 2022 is that **perturbing the MSA releases alternative conformations**, i.e., the default full MSA pins the network to one state.

- **del Alamo et al. 2022 [10]** (⚠ the briefing conflated this with the unrelated Keedy et al. PTP1B crystallography paper, eLife e07574; the real e75751 is del Alamo, Sala, Mchaourab & Meiler, "Sampling alternative conformational states of transporters and receptors with AlphaFold2," on transporters/GPCRs): "Whereas models of most proteins generated using the default AF2 pipeline are conformationally homogeneous and nearly identical to one another, reducing the depth of the input multiple sequence alignments by stochastic subsampling led to the generation of accurate models in multiple conformations" [10, Abstract]. "Decreasing the depth of the subsampled MSAs, by contrast, appeared to promote the generation of alternative conformations in most proteins" [10, Results].
- **Stein & Mchaourab 2022 (SPEACH_AF) [11]** (⚠ issue is 18(8), not 18(10)): "a general approach to drive Alphafold2 to model alternate protein conformations through simple manipulation of the multiple sequence alignment via in silico mutagenesis" [11, Abstract]. Importantly, they report MSA-depth subsampling *failed* for proteins with a single training-set structure: "decreasing the number of sequences in the MSA did not lead to the alternate conformations for these proteins" [11, Results] — an honest boundary condition on the subsampling result, and evidence that training-set memorization (§3.3) can dominate MSA effects.
- **Wayment-Steele et al. 2024 (AF-Cluster) [12]**: "clustering a multiple-sequence alignment by sequence similarity enables AlphaFold2 to sample alternative states of known metamorphic proteins with high confidence" [12, Abstract]. The cleanest pinning evidence is in the figure: "The highest-confidence regions of the AF-Cluster distribution for KaiBTE are within 3 Å r.m.s.d. of crystal structures of both the ground and FS state. By contrast, sampling the MSA uniformly returns only the FS state with high confidence" [12, Fig. 1 caption] — the full MSA selects one of two real states.
- **AFsample2 (Kalakoti & Wallner 2025 [13])**: "a method using random MSA column masking to reduce co-evolutionary signals, enhancing structural diversity in AF2-generated models" [13, Abstract]; "The amount of MSA masking, i.e., the fraction of randomized positions, was observed to be the most important factor in the ability of the inference system to generate alternate conformational states" [13, Results].
- **AFsample (Wallner 2023 [14])** (⚠ does *not* subsample the MSA): improvement comes from "stochastically perturbing the neural network by enabling dropout at inference combined with massive sampling" [14, Abstract], and "the sole reason for the improved performance of AFsample is improved sampling as AFsample was using identical MSAs as the AlphaFold-Multimer v2 baseline" [14, Results]. This is the useful negative control of the family: network stochasticity alone moves multimer predictions, so not all state-switching is MSA-caused — but the conformational-diversity results of [10,12,13] specifically require MSA perturbation.
- **ColabFold (Mirdita et al. 2022 [20])** contributes a different angle — the *covariation* channel is often dispensable even for complexes: "In AlphaFold2 we find the same to be true, although surprisingly the paired alignment is often not needed (Fig. 2c)" [20, main text], though pairing does help "only if orthologous genes are paired with each other" [20, Methods].

### 3.2 State bias in GPCRs, kinases, and apo/holo pairs

- **Heo & Feig 2022 [19]** (⚠ Proteins 90(11):1873–1885, not 90(12):2043; ⚠ their protocol *removes* MSA features rather than thinning them): "AlphaFold2 only predicts one state and is biased toward either the active or inactive conformation depending on the GPCR class" [19, Abstract]. Removing the MSA plus state-annotated templates fixes it: "when MSA input features were removed and state-annotated GPCR databases were used, it became possible to generate highly accurate models for both active and inactive state structures of GPCRs … the activation state-annotated structural template databases could be used to guide the AF2 modeling network towards a specific activation state based on homology, but without interference from MSA-based contacts that are still biased by conformational state preferences according to training" [19, Results & Discussion]; the protocol runs "without MSA input features to avoid learned biases towards inactive GPCR states" [19, Conclusions]. The phrase "MSA-based contacts … biased by conformational state preferences according to training" is a direct statement that the MSA channel carries the training-set's state bias into the pair representation.
- **Sala et al. 2023 [18]** (⚠ Front. Mol. Biosci. 10:1121962, not 1134613; authors are Sala, Hildebrand & Meiler — no Carlsson): their benchmark targets "class A and one class B1 GPCRs for which inactive structures were more prevalent than active ones in the AF2 training set. Furthermore, the inactive structure of ADRB1 was directly part of the AF2 training set, thus representing a very strong bias" [18, Discussion]; only their method "and noMSA were the only methods able to overcome the ADRB1 inactive state bias" [18, Results] — again, removing the MSA removes the bias.
- **Saldaño et al. 2022 [17]** (⚠ Bioinformatics 38(10):2742–2748, not 38(16):3934–3940): "AlphaFold2 predicts the holo form of a protein in ∼70% of the cases, being unable to reproduce the observed conformational diversity with the same error for both conformers" [17, Abstract]; "most of the proteins are modelled with a bias towards a given conformer" [17, Results]; "only 2% of the proteins showed models similar to either the holo or the apo forms with similar error" [17, Discussion]. ⚠ The briefing's "biased toward the most populated/crystallized conformation" framing is **rejected by the paper itself**: "exploring the BioLip database to estimate the relative presence of apo and holo forms in PDB shows about 64% of apo forms" — apo is the *more* crystallized form, yet AF2 prefers holo. The authors attribute the bias instead to the MSA's evolutionary consensus averaging over structurally heterogeneous family members (coevolutionary signals reflect "the consensus ones, independently of the structural variations among family members" [17, Results/Discussion, paraphrased from the family-heterogeneity analysis]). This attribution — the MSA writes a **family consensus** that erases within-family structural variation — is precisely the mechanism we measure directly in §5.

### 3.3 Memorization and fold switching

- **Chakravarty & Porter 2022 [16]** (⚠ Protein Science 31(6):e4353, not J Mol Biol 434:167773): "Overall, 94% of AlphaFold2 predictions captured one experimentally determined conformation but not the other" [16, Abstract]; "AlphaFold2's high prediction confidences for fold switchers indicate that it uses sophisticated pattern recognition to search for one most probable conformer rather than protein biophysics to model a protein's structural ensemble" [16, Abstract].
- **Chakravarty et al. 2024 [15]**: "We find that (1) AF is a weak predictor of fold switching and (2) some of its successes result from memorization of training-set structures rather than learned protein energetics" [15, Abstract]. The mechanism statement is striking for an MSA-centric network: "This memorization can be so strong that AF2 uses it to inform predictions instead of coevolutionary information detected by its Evoformer" [15, Introduction] — i.e., the PDB-memorization channel can *override* the MSA coevolution channel.

### 3.4 The designers' own admission

The AF3 paper states the bias plainly: "A key limitation of protein structure prediction models is that they typically predict static structures as seen in the PDB, not the dynamical behaviour of biomolecular systems in solution" [2, Discussion, p6], with a concrete example: "E3 ubiquitin ligases natively adopt an open conformation in an apo state and have been observed only in a closed state when bound to ligands, but AF3 exclusively predicts the closed state for both holo and apo systems" [2, p6]. This is the same PDB-most-likely training objective AF2 declared openly: "AlphaFold is trained to produce the protein structure most likely to appear as part of a PDB structure" [1, p6].

The sharpest external summary is the RSC Medicinal Chemistry review (Vargas-Rosales & Caflisch 2025 [23]; ⚠ the working DOI is 10.1039/d4md00869c): "The MSA is so important to AlphaFold2, that it has been pointed out that AlphaFold2 has learnt a MSA–structure relationship, not a sequence–structure relationship as claimed" [23, §2.1]. Their reference for this claim (ref. 71, identified as requested) is **OpenFold** (Ahdritz et al. 2024 [24]): "However, it merits noting that AlphaFold2 in general learns MSA–structure, not sequence–structure, relationships" [24, Discussion], with the corollary that "the applicability of the AlphaFold2 architecture to problems that do not exhibit a coevolutionary signal remains undemonstrated" [24, Discussion].

**Synthesis of §3.** The evidence that deep MSAs pin predictors to single, crystallographic states is strong and multi-pronged: subsampling/thinning/clustering/masking the MSA releases alternative states [10,11,12,13]; removing the MSA removes learned inactive-state bias in GPCRs [18,19]; the default pipeline captures one of two known conformers 94% of the time for fold switchers [16], partly via outright memorization that bypasses the Evoformer [15]; and the vendors themselves concede the static/PDB-most-likely character of the output [1,2]. The bias is a joint product of (i) the MSA's family-consensus statistics and (ii) a PDB-distribution training target — and memorization can dominate both [15,17].

## 4. Counter-evidence: the MSA carries real evolutionary statistics

The bias results must not be read as "the MSA is noise." Four lines of evidence show it carries genuine, sometimes irreplaceable, evolutionary information.

**(a) Depth dependence in the predictors themselves.** AF2 accuracy "decreases substantially when the median alignment depth is less than around 30 sequences" and saturates beyond ~100 [1, p6; p8]; AF3 shows "a very similar dependence on MSA depth" [2, p5]. ESMFold's own comparison shows MSA-backed AF2 ahead on CAMEO/CASP14 (TM 0.88/0.85 vs ESMFold 0.83/0.68) [3, p6], and ESM3's Table S9 shows the MSA advantage concentrated on hard targets (CASP14 gap +0.133 LDDT) [4, p44].

**(b) ProteinGym's MSA-depth stratification.** Notin et al. 2023 (ProteinGym [21]; ⚠ there is no arXiv version — it is a NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks paper) report zero-shot DMS performance by MSA depth in Table A6 ("Zero-shot substitution DMS benchmark by MSA depth … Low: Neff/L <1; Medium: 1< Neff/L <100; High: Neff/L >100" [21, Table A6 caption, Appendix A.5]). ⚠ The briefing's implicit claim that MSA-based methods collapse at shallow depth while language models hold up is **not what the table shows**: the pure-coevolution method EVE scores 0.425 / 0.453 / 0.481 (Low/Medium/High) and the alignment-based GEMME "achieves the best performance in several categories, such as … low and medium depth MSAs" [21, §5.1] — alignment-based methods are *among the strongest* at shallow depth, while language models also improve with depth (ESM-1v 0.326 at Low depth, rising to 0.502 at High [21, Table A6]). The defensible reading is the opposite of a simple "MSA = bias, LM = robust" story: **MSA statistics are a consistently useful substrate for variant-effect prediction across depths**, and the "robustness to MSA depth" framing in the literature originates with Tranception (Notin et al. 2022 [25]), not ProteinGym itself.

**(c) Pure MSA coevolution beats language models on viral fitness.** In ESM3's own benchmark, "ESM3-open performs substantially worse than EVMutation … on viral fitness prediction, while being competitive with ESM2 … on non-viral fitness prediction" [4, Fig. S24D caption, p69], and pre-mitigation, "ESM3-small outperforms ESM2 on viral landscapes, but falls short of EVMutation" [4, Appendix, p70]. EVMutation is a Markov-random-field model built purely on MSA coevolution. (Caveat: ESM3-open's viral deficit is amplified by the viral-sequence denylist used for open release — "Viral fitness prediction was substantially impacted by the data mitigation" [4, Fig. S24D caption] — but the ESM3-small vs EVMutation gap predates mitigation.) On rapidly evolving viral families, per-family MSA statistics outperform generic sequence priors.

**(d) MSA modeling specifically rescues variant-effect prediction at scale.** MSA Pairformer (Akiyama et al. 2025 [22]), a 111M-parameter MSA language model built on AF3's MSA module, documents a scaling trade-off that runs *against* single-sequence embeddings: "Unlike single-sequence models that deteriorate in variant effect prediction as they scale, MSA Pairformer maintains strong performance in both tasks" [22, Abstract]; "While scaling single-sequence models results in better unsupervised contact prediction, the opposite has been found in variant effect prediction … the performance of ESM2 models peaks with 650M parameters, decreases for both 3B and 15B" [22, §4.4]; "MSA Pairformer uniquely achieves strong performance in both contact prediction and variant effect prediction" [22, §4.4]. For a DMS prediction project this is the most consequential counter-evidence: **the evolutionary statistics carried by MSA processing are precisely the signal that single-sequence embeddings lose**, and variant-effect prediction is where MSA information pays off.

**Synthesis of §4.** The MSA is simultaneously (i) a real information source — depth-dependent structure accuracy [1,2,4], competitive fitness prediction at all depths [21], superior viral fitness prediction [4], and a variant-effect signal that embeddings cannot scale into [22] — and (ii) a biasing channel that pins outputs to family-consensus / PDB-most-likely states (§3). Any account of the MSA's role in an AF3-style trunk must explain both facts at once.

## 5. The gap — and what our project measures

### 5.1 What nobody has measured

The literature reviewed above establishes *that* the MSA biases structure outputs (§3) and *that* it carries real evolutionary signal (§4), but all existing measurements are at the **input→output** level (perturb the MSA, look at predicted structures or fitness correlations). Four questions remain open:

- **(a) Representation-level quantification.** No study quantifies the MSA's effect *inside* the trunk — on the pair representation itself — in terms of effective rank, MSA-vs-no-MSA representational similarity (CKA), or preservation of mutation-conditioned directions. Structure-level metrics (RMSD, pLDDT) and fitness correlations cannot distinguish "the MSA adds information" from "the MSA rescales/suppresses existing variation."
- **(b) Channel decomposition.** The MSA-perturbation family perturbs the whole MSA object. Nobody has decomposed the effect into **shared context** (any set of sequences), **per-column conservation**, and **covariation** channels. (ColabFold's paired-alignment result [20] and AFsample2's column-masking result [13] are partial, output-level hints.)
- **(c) Write-once dynamics.** AF3's architecture implies the MSA enters the pair representation once ("the MSA representation is not retained and all information passes through the pair representation" [2, p2]), but nobody has tested this causally — e.g., by gating MSA injection to specific recycles and asking whether the trunk can later self-correct.
- **(d) Net functional effect on mutation prediction with correct controls.** ProteinGym stratifies by MSA depth across *methods* [21]; it does not ablate the MSA inside one fixed trunk and measure per-mutant prediction with depth-matched baselines. RF3 itself reports no MSA ablation at all [5, p18].

### 5.2 Our corrected findings (RF3 pair representation, with vs without MSA)

⚠ **Retraction note.** Our June 2026 extraction of Z_II-with-MSA was artifacted (all files sliced at position 0); the earlier claims of "rank collapse 231→30" and "supervised −40%" are invalid and retracted internally. The corrected numbers below are from the 2026-07-25 re-extraction [26].

Using RF3 (an open AF3 implementation [5]) on RBD with a 15-row shallow MSA (GOLD_MSA):

- **Geometry (E0).** With MSA, the pair representation's effective rank drops 181→139 (−23%); CKA(Z_msa, Z_no) = 0.568; per-mutant delta directions are preserved at ~0.48. So the MSA's geometric effect is a **partial subspace compression with substantial representational continuity** — not the near-total erasure the artifacted June extraction had suggested.
- **Function.** Supervised cross-position Spearman *improves* 0.414→0.638 (+0.22; +0.15 over the honest depth-1 baseline). The MSA's net functional effect on DMS prediction is **positive** here — consistent with the counter-evidence in §4(b,d) and opposite to the retracted −40% claim.
- **Dose × content (E1 arms: depth01/04/08/full15, colshuffle, consensus, rowshuffle, random).** Rank collapse has a **generic shared-context component** — even random sequences collapse rank to ~150 — but the **functional gain comes only from real biological content**, mostly **per-column conservation** (colshuffle retains ≈2/3 of the gain), with **covariation secondary**; the effect **saturates at ~8 sequences**, echoing AF2's ~30-sequence threshold [1, p6] at an even shallower depth.
- **Write-once dynamics (E7, recycle gating).** One recycle of MSA exposure installs ~75% of the geometric effect and ~85% of the functional gain; the trunk **cannot self-correct afterwards** — a direct causal confirmation of AF3's "not retained / all information passes through the pair representation" design [2, p2] at the functional level.
- **Framing.** In AF3-style trunks the MSA→Z channel is a family-consensus mean (OuterProductMean over rows — the only MSA→Z channel in RF3 [5]): a one-shot "retrieval-key / family-consensus write" that re-encodes mutation signals toward family evolutionary statistics — which is exactly what cross-position DMS generalization needs. This mechanism is the representation-level version of what Saldaño et al. inferred at the output level (the MSA writes "the consensus … independently of the structural variations among family members" [17]) and what Heo & Feig called "MSA-based contacts … biased by conformational state preferences according to training" [19]. It reconciles §3 and §4: the same family-consensus write that biases conformational sampling is the evolutionary-statistics injection that improves mutation-effect prediction.

To our knowledge this is the first measurement of (a)–(d) for any AF3-style trunk.

## 7. Verification notes (briefing corrections)

1. `s41586-018-0509-0.pdf` = Dou et al. 2018 β-barrel design [7], not AF1. AF1 = Senior et al. 2020 [8].
2. eLife 75751 is del Alamo, **Sala, Mchaourab & Meiler** (transporters/GPCRs) [10] — the briefing's "Nussinov/Fraser, PTP1B" attribution conflates it with Keedy et al., eLife 4:e07574 (2015, XFEL crystallography of PTP1B; no AF2).
3. SPEACH_AF [11] is PLoS Comput Biol **18(8)**:e1010483; its method is MSA *mutagenesis*, not identity filtering, and it reports MSA-depth subsampling failing on single-structure-family targets.
4. AFsample [14] perturbs the *network* (dropout) with **identical MSAs**; MSA randomization is AFsample2 [13] (column masking; Commun. Biol. 8:373, two authors: Kalakoti & Wallner).
5. Chakravarty & Porter 2022 [16] is **Protein Science 31(6):e4353**, not J Mol Biol 434:167773.
6. Saldaño et al. [17] is **Bioinformatics 38(10):2742–2748**; the paper *rejects* the "most populated/crystallized" explanation (apo is 64% of BioLip yet holo is preferred) and attributes the bias to MSA/coevolutionary consensus over heterogeneous families.
7. Sala et al. 2023 [18] is **Front. Mol. Biosci. 10:1121962**, authors Sala, Hildebrand & Meiler (no Carlsson).
8. Heo & Feig [19] is **Proteins 90(11):1873–1885**; the headline protocol *removes* MSA features entirely rather than thinning them.
9. ProteinGym [21] has no arXiv ID (NeurIPS 2023 D&B); Table A6 verified; the claim "MSA methods degrade at shallow depth while LMs hold up" is **not supported** — alignment-based methods (EVE, GEMME) are among the strongest at low depth; the robustness framing originates with Tranception [25].
10. MSA Pairformer [22] does not replace AF3's MSA module with embeddings; its trade-off runs the other way: *single-sequence* models sacrifice variant-effect prediction for contact accuracy at scale, the MSA model does both.
11. RSC Med. Chem. DOI is **10.1039/d4md00869c** [23]; ref 71 = OpenFold (Ahdritz et al. 2024, Nat. Methods 21:1514–1524) [24].
12. ColabFold's exact wording is "the paired alignment is often not needed" [20] — scoped to residue-index-mode complex prediction; the same paper notes pairing helps when orthologs are correctly paired.
